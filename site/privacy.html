Privacy

Chomugiri — the autonomous developer workspace.

Last updated 16 September 2026

The short version. There are no accounts, no analytics, and no database of ours holding your work. Your chats, your files and your API keys live on your device. What leaves it is what a task needs, sent to the service doing that task — and every one of those is named below.

Everything here is checkable. Chomugiri is open source at github.com/thanoxxinfnity/Champu; if any sentence on this page does not match the code, the code is right and this page is a bug.

The Android app and the web version are not the same

This matters more than anything else on the page, so it goes first.

  • The Android app carries the whole API surface natively — it runs a small HTTP server on the phone itself, on the loopback interface, which nothing outside the phone can reach. Requests to a model provider go from your phone straight to them. No server of ours is involved at any point.
  • The web version cannot do that. A browser is not allowed to call NVIDIA directly — NVIDIA sends no CORS headers — so requests pass through the Chomugiri server (/api/chat and friends), which forwards them and streams the answer back. It keeps no copy and writes nothing to a database. But it does see the request as it goes by, and so does whoever hosts it. If that is not acceptable to you, use the Android app, or run your own copy — it is the same source.

What stays on your device

  • Chats and generated files — in your browser's IndexedDB, or in the Android app's own private storage. Clearing the app's data deletes them, and nothing keeps a copy.
  • Settings — theme, model choice, custom endpoints.

Where your API keys are kept, precisely

Stated exactly rather than reassuringly, because "encrypted" is the easiest word in the world to put on a page:

  • In the Android app they are written to a private SharedPreferences file called chomugiri-secrets, in MODE_PRIVATE. That means no other app on the phone can read it, and it sits inside app storage which Android encrypts at rest along with everything else on a modern device. The app does not add an encryption layer of its own — it is not using EncryptedSharedPreferences or the hardware keystore. On an unlocked, rooted phone, or from a full device backup, those keys are readable.
  • In a browser they sit in the same local storage as the rest of the workspace, which is to say: as safe as that browser profile is, and no safer.

Either way they are sent only to the provider they belong to, and only when a request needs them. They are never sent to us — in the web version they pass through the gateway on their way to the provider, and are not stored there.

What we do not do

  • No analytics, telemetry, crash reporting or usage tracking. There is no code in the app that reports anything back.
  • No advertising, and no advertising identifiers.
  • No selling or sharing of anything, because nothing is held to sell.
  • No model of ours is trained on your prompts or your code. We do not have one.

What this page cannot promise

Two honest limits, because a policy that claims to cover other companies' behaviour is worth nothing:

  • Whether a provider trains on what you send it is their decision, not ours. Paid API tiers usually say they do not; free and anonymous ones often reserve the right to. Pollinations is the keyless default here precisely because it needs no account, and that is the trade. If this matters to your work, read the policy of the provider you have selected and prefer one you hold a key for.
  • A search query is a statement about what you are working on. Sending one tells that search engine something, and there is no way to ask a question of the internet privately. Research is off unless you ask for it.

Permissions the Android app asks for

All five, as declared in the manifest:

PermissionWhat it is for
INTERNETReaching the providers you have configured.
ACCESS_NETWORK_STATETelling you the request failed because you are offline, rather than just failing.

FOREGROUND_SERVICE_DATA_SYNC		Keeping a long build running when you switch away from the app. Android kills background work otherwise, and a compile that dies halfway is a compile you have to start again.
POST_NOTIFICATIONSTelling you that build finished. You can refuse it; everything else still works.

There is no storage permission, no camera, no microphone, no location and no contacts. Generated files are written to the app's own storage and handed to you through your browser's downloads, which needs no permission at all.

Voice recordings

The recordings in the app and on this site are ours, and they play from the app. They are deliberately not built into anything Chomugiri generates — a game you export carries none of them, so what you publish is yours.

Children

Chomugiri is a developer tool and is not directed at children under 13. Nothing is collected from anyone, of any age.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it. There is no mailing list to notify, because there are no addresses to notify.

Contact

Questions, or a claim on this page you think is wrong: open an issue on the repository. That is the only channel, and it is a public one.

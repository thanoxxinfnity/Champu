Chomugiri is an autonomous developer workspace. You describe a thing — an Android app, a Minecraft add-on, a Godot game, a deck, a website — and it plans it, builds it, compiles it, and hands you the artifact. Not a snippet. The actual signed APK, the actual .mcaddon, the actual playable build.

Two games, both built by the pipeline

Neither of these was written by hand. Both came out of Chomugiri's own planner, scene builder and exporter, from one sentence each — which is the only way to know the thing works.

ChomuCity.apk — 33 MB. An open-world block: walk it in third person with the camera orbiting behind you, get into one of three parked cars and drive it, and run four jobs around the map. There is no gun, deliberately — a third-person shooting rig is a different problem, and half of one is worse than none.

Landscape. Left half of the screen is the movement stick, right half turns the camera, USE gets you in and out of a car.

ChomuGame.apk — 33 MB. A first-person zombie survival shooter over the same city block. Five missions, each unlocking the next: clear the yard, hold out ninety seconds, run for supplies, defend the generator, last stand. Waves that grow, a hitscan rifle with ammo and reloads, health that comes back when you break off. The horde paths round the buildings rather than through them.

FIRE, RELOAD and JUMP sit under your right thumb; shove the stick to its edge to sprint.

Both are signed, but not by the Play Store, so Android will warn that they came from an unknown source; allow the install from your browser and they run. The crates and barrels are real models from Poly Haven, CC0. Exported with Godot 4.7.2.

How it runs

  • On your device. The workspace is a Next.js app, and the Android build carries the whole API surface natively — it serves itself on the phone's loopback interface, so requests go from your phone to the provider with no server of ours in between. The web version cannot do that, because a browser is not allowed to call NVIDIA directly; there, requests pass through a gateway that forwards and stores nothing.
  • With your keys. You paste them in Settings, they stay in the app's own private storage on the phone, and they go only to the provider they belong to. The privacy policy says exactly which file and exactly how well protected — no rounder than that.
  • Nothing collected. No accounts, no analytics, no database of ours. See the privacy policy for exactly what leaves the device and where it goes.

The voices

Chomugiri can speak. These are the workspace voices — the same recordings that ship in the app under Settings → Voice. They belong to the app and to this page. They are not baked into anything Chomugiri generates for you: a game it builds ships silent of these, so the thing you make is yours to license as you like.
